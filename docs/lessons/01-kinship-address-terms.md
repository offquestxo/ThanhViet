# Lesson 1 — Kinship (Address Terms, First Pass)

*Built per [Curriculum-Lesson-Design-Framework.md](../Curriculum-Lesson-Design-Framework.md)'s Section 18 lesson-document template, and citing that document's numbering throughout ("Framework Section N" — not PRD.md's or TAD.md's, which reuse the same digits for unrelated sections). Companion visual spec: [Visual-Primitives-Reference.md](../Visual-Primitives-Reference.md).*

## Lesson Purpose

Establish the foundational Vietnamese address-term system — the relationship-dependent logic that governs how speakers refer to each other and to themselves — so that every later module (Greetings, Survival Phrases, Food, Travel, etc.) can use natural, socially correct language from the start. This lesson is functionally the pronoun system, taught through kinship terms because that is how Vietnamese encodes it.

## Learner Outcome

After this lesson, the learner can:
- Select the correct address term for a person based on relative age, gender, and generation.
- Correctly self-reference using the mirrored term when addressed by someone else.
- Recognize six address terms by ear and identify who each refers to.
- Actively produce four of those terms in guided and novel contexts.

## Prerequisites

None. This is the first lesson of the Introductory course and the learner's first exposure to Vietnamese language content, tone-focused activities excepted (dropped for this course per current scope).

## New Language

**Active vocabulary (taught for production):**

| Term | Refers to | Self-reference use |
|---|---|---|
| anh | Older male, same generation (peer) | Used by that person to refer to themselves when speaking to someone younger |
| chị | Older female, same generation (peer) | Used by that person to refer to themselves when speaking to someone younger |
| em | Younger person, same generation, either gender | Used by that person to refer to themselves when speaking to someone older |
| cô | Adult woman, one generation up | Not taught as self-reference at this level |

**Recognition-only vocabulary (seen/heard, not drilled for production):**

| Term | Refers to |
|---|---|
| chú | Adult man, one generation up |
| bác | Older adult, either gender, one generation up |
| ông | Elderly man / grandfather generation |
| bà | Elderly woman / grandmother generation |

`ông` and `bà` are introduced only through listening and retrieval examples, not the primary Character Relationship Scene, to keep the first visual uncrowded.

**Deferred:** `bạn` (hierarchy-neutral "friend" term) and its self-reference pairing (`mình`/`tôi`) — held for Lesson 2 so the age/generation pattern isn't diluted while it's first being established.

## Previously Learned Language Recycled

None — this is Lesson 1. This lesson is the recycling *source* for the rest of the course, starting with Greetings (Lesson 2), which should reuse `anh/chị/em/cô` immediately and often.

## Concepts Introduced

The Vietnamese address-term system runs on two variables: **relative age/generation** and **gender** — with `em` as the one exception that doesn't split by gender. Same-generation relationships are ranked by who is older; a one-generation gap shifts to a different term set entirely. Critically, the correct term is a property of the *relationship between speakers*, not a fixed label on either person — the same person may be `anh` to one speaker and `em` to another, and self-reference mirrors this (whichever term you're addressed as becomes the term you use for yourself in return).

**[GRAMMAR OPPORTUNITY]** — This lesson surfaces the seed of third-person reference (needing to describe someone outside the conversation) but does not teach it. Flagged for Lesson 2 alongside the `ấy` suffix system, pending the external grammar methodology.

## Visual Design

**Primary primitive:** Character Relationship Scene (full spec: [Visual-Primitives-Reference.md](../Visual-Primitives-Reference.md)).

**Scene composition:** Learner avatar at center (non-lexical anchor — no Vietnamese word used as the "you" label). Six figures arranged radially: four active-vocabulary figures (older male, older female peer, younger person, older woman) fully colored and tappable; two recognition-only figures (older man, older-adult-either-gender) visually dimmed and marked recognition-only, non-tappable for production. `ông` and `bà` do not appear in this scene.

**Interaction sequence (revised from primitive draft — see note below):**
1. Learner taps a figure.
2. Learner hears the Vietnamese term only — no label yet.
3. Learner is prompted to infer meaning from the figure's visual age/gender cues and audio alone.
4. Learner interacts further (tries another figure, compares).
5. Pattern-noticing moment — learner starts predicting terms before tapping.
6. Vietnamese text label appears as confirmation, on a second tap or explicit reveal — not simultaneously with first audio.
7. Learner toggles their own avatar's age/gender attribute; all figure labels re-evaluate live, demonstrating that terms are relationship-dependent, not fixed identities.

**Note:** This delayed-label sequence is a correction to the Character Relationship Scene primitive's current documented interaction pattern (which shows label + audio simultaneously on first tap). This lesson uses the corrected sequence; the primitive spec should be updated to match rather than treated as a one-off exception.

**Secondary use — Concept Visual step:** The two-generation-bucket concept map (same generation / one generation up) is shown *after* the interactive scene and pattern-noticing stage, as the formalization payoff, not the introduction.

**Vocabulary governance check:** Background scene elements (street, trees, houses) contain no nameable vocabulary the learner is expected to notice or recall.

## Listening & Discrimination

Tone-specific discrimination is dropped for this course. Listening focus here is on **word-to-meaning mapping**: hearing each of the six terms in isolation and in short phrases, and correctly associating the sound with who is being referred to, without relying on text.

## Shadowing

Learner repeats each of the four active terms after the native model, in isolation and within a short address phrase (e.g. a greeting fragment). Repetition here targets general pronunciation and listening familiarity, not tone accuracy, consistent with this course's current scope.

## Tone / Pronunciation Tuning

Not applicable for this lesson. Tone-focused activities are dropped for the Introductory course per current scope. **Curriculum Note:** vocabulary introduced here will require a dedicated tone-retrofit pass once Tone Tuner is built (see Curriculum Notes below).

## Reading / Recognition

Learner sees each of the six terms in written form only after having heard and inferred them through the Character Relationship Scene — writing is introduced as confirmation, not as the first encounter. Diacritics are present but not explicitly explained in this lesson (no meta-discussion of the writing system yet).

## Writing / Encoding

Short, purposeful writing tied to scenarios rather than copying drills: learner is shown a scenario (e.g. an older woman, clearly a different generation) and writes the correct term, forcing retrieval at the point of writing. Limited to the four active terms.

## Meaning & Comprehensible Input

Meaning is established entirely through the Character Relationship Scene and example scenarios — visual age/gender/generation cues plus native audio — with no English glosses at any point in the visual layer. Five teaching scenarios establish the initial pattern (see Guided Practice and Retrieval & Review below for the full scenario inventory).

## Pattern Noticing

Learner is expected to begin predicting the correct term *before* being told it, once several scenarios have been seen — this is the explicit goal of the delayed-label interaction sequence. The two-axis logic (age/generation, gender) should be something the learner starts to sense through repeated exposure before the concept map ever confirms it explicitly.

## Grammar Connection

Deferred to the external grammar methodology once provided. This lesson's [GRAMMAR OPPORTUNITY] tag (third-person reference) is noted above for future integration.

## Guided Practice

Three guided production scenarios, each requiring the learner to complete a short chunk (address term + self-reference term) rather than produce an isolated word:
1. Learner is the younger person, speaking to an older man → produces `anh` (address) + `em` (self-reference).
2. Learner is the older person (a woman), speaking to a younger man → produces `em` (address) + `chị` (self-reference).
3. Learner is shown a description of an older, non-peer-generation woman and completes a greeting using `cô`.

## Retrieval & Review

**Listener decoding (3 scenarios)** — learner hears someone else's greeting directed at them (e.g. "Anh chào em") and must correctly identify who said what and how to self-refer in response, without flipping the mapping.

**Retrieval checks (4 scenarios)** — novel situations, concept map not visible, surface details varied so the learner must apply the underlying rule rather than recall a memorized scenario. Example: a woman slightly older than the learner, met for the first time at a community gathering — which term, and what does the learner call themselves?

## Mastery Check

**4-item mastery pool** — fully novel scenarios, mixed address-term and self-reference production, covering all four active terms at least once across the set, in varied settings (market, neighbor, casual meeting, community context) so success requires applying the rule rather than remembering a specific example. Observable criterion: learner produces the correct term pair (address + self-reference) consistently across the pool, without the concept map visible.

## Native Audio Requirements

**[NATIVE AUDIO REQUIRED]**
- Isolated-word recordings: all 8 terms (`anh, chị, em, cô, chú, bác, ông, bà`).
- Short example-sentence recordings: 2–3 per active term (`anh, chị, em, cô`), each demonstrating the term in a brief address/greeting-style phrase.
- Recognition-only terms (`chú, bác, ông, bà`) require isolated-word audio only — they appear naturally in listening/retrieval input but are not drilled for production, so no dedicated sentence sets are needed for them at this stage.
- No tone-contrast or minimal-pair recordings required under current course scope.

## Curriculum Notes

- This lesson is the recycling source for the entire course — Lesson 2 (Greetings) and beyond should reuse `anh/chị/em/cô` immediately and often; do not reintroduce these terms as if new.
- `bạn` and its self-reference pairing (`mình`/`tôi`) are deferred to Lesson 2, alongside third-person reference (`ấy` forms). Lesson 2 should also decide where `bạn` sits relative to the anh/chị/em hierarchy system — as a fallback for unclear age, or a deliberate stylistic alternative — before it is taught.
- `chú` and `bác` will eventually need promotion from recognition-only to active status in a later Kinship lesson; when that happens, they require the same solidifying treatment (retrieval variation, delayed mastery check) as the current active set — this was intentionally not built for them here.
- All vocabulary in this lesson was acquired without tone-focused instruction. A tone-retrofit pass is required once Tone Tuner exists, before this vocabulary is considered fully mastered per the framework's broader tone philosophy (Framework Section 4).
- The Character Relationship Scene primitive's documented interaction pattern should be updated to reflect this lesson's delayed-label sequence (see Visual Design note above), rather than treating it as a lesson-specific exception.
- The `bác`/`cô` boundary for addressing an older woman (closeness/formality-dependent) remains undefined and should be resolved before either term is taught with more precision in a later lesson.
